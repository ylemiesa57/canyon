You send

  • The 3D modelWhatever your CAD program saves. You do not have to prepare anything.
  • The drawingIf you have one. Canyon reads the dimensions and the notes off it.
  • Your termsHow you pay, what certifications you need. Set once, applied to everything after.

Canyon sends back

  • A price$88 to $104 a part at 250. The range tightens as real shops confirm it.
  • A dateA lead time checked against how booked those shops actually are.
  • Why it costs thatMost of it is machine time. One wall is thinner than it needs to be: thicken it a little and this order drops $3,725.

You never check Canyon's work. You say how many you want and when you need them, and that is the whole job.

What lands

  • The whole threadSix files and four emails. Canyon reads them together instead of one at a time.
  • A model, a drawingSize, features and every note on the print, pulled off both and matched up.
  • The contradictionThe drawing said Rev A and the email said Rev B. Canyon caught it and held the quote.

What comes out

  • Your real costEach step priced on the machine that would run it, at the rate you set. Not an industry average.
  • Every quantity$54.32 for one. $43.18 each at ten. $39.21 each at fifty.
  • A quote to signFour minutes after it landed. Change a number if you want, then send it.

Your rates and your floor never leave the shop. Canyon quotes with them. The buyer only ever sees a price.
